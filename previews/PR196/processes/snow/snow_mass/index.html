Snow mass balance

Warning

This page is a work in progress. If you have any questions or notice any errors, please raise an issue.

Overview

The snow water equivalent $W_\text{snow}$ (m) evolves according to a continuous mass balance,

\[\begin{equation} \frac{\partial W_\text{snow}}{\partial t} = P_s + R_\text{on snow} - M_r - E_\text{subl}, \end{equation}\]

where $P_s$ is snowfall, $R_\text{on snow} = f_\text{snow}\,R$ is the rainfall intercepted by the snow-covered fraction, $M_r$ is the meltwater outflow draining from the snowpack base, and $E_\text{subl}$ is the surface sublimation rate. In the coupled land model the bare-ground fraction $(1 - f_\text{snow})$ of the rainfall reaches the soil directly and the meltwater outflow is added to it, forming the water input to the Surface runoff scheme.

Meltwater outflow

Liquid water in excess of the capillary retention drains from the snowpack with a Darcy-type cubic conductivity ([4], eqns 23–24, in excess-saturation form).

Terrarium.compute_meltwater_outflow — Function
compute_meltwater_outflow(
    hydraulics::ConstantSnowHydraulics,
    θ_liq
) -> Any

Darcy-type meltwater outflow M_r (m/s, SWE). Liquid water in excess of the capillary retention L_c drains from the snowpack with a cubic conductivity [4] (in excess-saturation form): M_r = K_sat · S*³ with S* = max(θ_liq − L_c, 0) / (1 − L_c), where θ_liq is the liquid fraction of the water substance. Outflow vanishes smoothly as θ_liq → L_c and saturates at K_sat as θ_liq → 1.

Implicit meltwater drainage

With the default saturated conductivity of 20 m/h, a fully melted pack drains its liquid water within seconds, so an explicit step of useful length drains more water than the pack holds as liquid. Since meltwater leaves at the zero-enthalpy reference of the closure (liquid water at 0 °C), the drained ice then leaves its latent deficit in the remaining pack, which cools far below the air temperature. Under an IMEX timestepper, snow_water_equivalent is therefore integrated implicitly (see Implicit-explicit time stepping), and the drainage is excluded from its explicit tendency.

Drainage changes neither the snow energy $\bar{U}$ nor the ice content $W_i = (1 - \theta_\text{liq}) W = -\bar{U} / (\rho_w L_f)$. With $W^+$ and $\bar{U}^+$ from the explicit stage (snowfall, rain, and sublimation), the backward Euler step of the drainage is a scalar equation per column, written in the liquid fraction $\theta$ via $W = W_i / (1 - \theta)$:

\[G(\theta) = \frac{W_i}{1 - \theta} + \Delta t\, M_r(\theta) - W^+ = 0.\]

$G$ is increasing and convex, so each Picard iteration takes a Newton step in $\theta$, which approaches the root from above: no iterate drains more than the backward Euler solution, at any step and any number of iterations. The system of each iteration is solved by a DiagonalOperator for snow_liquid_fraction. A fully liquid pack ($W_i = 0$) drains at the constant rate $M_r(1)$ until it is empty, so its step takes the exact solution $\max(W^+ - \Delta t\, M_r(1), 0)$.

The drainage of each step is stored in the auxiliary variable meltwater_flux, which the surface hydrology of a LandModel reads as the meltwater input to the soil in the following step. Snow and soil water are thus conserved, with the meltwater input lagged by one step. Under explicit integration, meltwater_flux is the outflow at the beginning of the step.

Diagonal G′(θᵐ) and right-hand side G′(θᵐ) θᵐ − G(θᵐ) at grid cell i, j of the Newton step for the liquid fraction, with G(θ) = W_i / (1 − θ) + Δt M_r(θ) − W⁺, the ice content W_i (compute_snow_ice_content), and W⁺ = explicit_water. The coefficients are the identity for a pack without ice or snow, which compute_implicit_snow_water_update! treats separately.

compute_implicit_snow_water_update!(
    out,
    i,
    j,
    grid,
    fields,
    explicit_water,
    snow::SingleLayerSnow{NF},
    constants::PhysicalConstants,

)

Snow water equivalent W = W_i / (1 − θ) at grid cell i, j from the solved liquid fraction θ = snow_liquid_fraction, bounded by θ⁺ so that W ≤ W⁺ = explicit_water, and the drainage meltwater_flux = (W⁺ − W) / Δt. A pack without ice (W_i = 0) takes the exact solution W = max(W⁺ − Δt M_r(1), 0) of its step, and a step without snow (W⁺ ≤ 0) leaves W = W⁺.

Snow water equivalent (SWE) tendency (m/s) at grid cell i, j:

dW_snow/dt = S + R_snow − M − E_subl

where S is snowfall, R_snow = f_snow · rainfall the rain intercepted by the snow-covered fraction, M the Darcy meltwater outflow (see snow_meltwater_flux), and E_subl the sublimation rate. M is omitted under implicit integration of snow_water_equivalent, where the implicit step drains the meltwater.
