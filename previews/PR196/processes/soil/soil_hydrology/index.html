Soil hydrology

Overview

Soil hydrology processes characterize the dynamics of ground water in both saturated and unsaturated soil. It defines parameters and methods needed to compute water fluxes between layers and grid cells within the soil domain. Implementations should extend AbstractSoilHydrology and should generally consist of at least four components:

  • A scheme for computing vertical water fluxes between soil layers
  • A closure parameterization linking soil saturation and pressure head
  • A parameterization for soil hydraulic properties
  • A forcing term representing user-defined, internal sources/sinks in the soil domain (not including evapotranspiration)

Static soil hydrology ("No Flow")

The simplest possible soil hydrology scheme is one in which the soil saturation state remains constant over time. This can be appropriate for simulations in regions where the soil is normally waterlogged or where changes in the hydrological state can be otherwise assumed to play a negligible role.

Richardson-Richards equation for variably saturated flow

The vertical flow of water in porous media, such as soils, can be formulated as following the conservation law

\[ \phi\frac{\partial \xi(\psi)}{\partial t} = - \boldsymbol{\nabla} \cdot \textbf{j}_{\text{w}} + F_{\text{w}}(z,t),\]

where (as defined in Soil stratigraphy) $\phi$ is the natural porosity (or saturated water content) of the soil volume and $\xi \in [0,1]$ the saturation of pore water/ice. $F_{\text{w}}(z,t)$ is an inhomogeneous source/sink (forcing) term (1/s) and $\textbf{j}_{\text{w}}$ the water flux vector (m/s).

Vertical fluxes in the soil column can be represented by combining gravity-driven advection with Darcy's law

\[\begin{equation} \textbf{j}_{\text{w}}^{\mathrm{v}} =\textbf{j}_{\text{w}} \cdot \mathbf{n}_z = -\kappa_{\text{w}}\frac{\partial \left(\psi + z\right)}{\partial z}, \end{equation}\]

where $\psi$ is the matric potential (m), $\mathbf{n}_z$ the normal vector perpendicular to the surface and $\kappa_{\text{w}}$ the hydraulic conductivity (m/s). Given the positive upwards convention (see Numerical core), the $z-$axis is thus defined positive away from the surface. Substituting this equation into the aforementioned conservation law yields the widely known Richardson-Richards equation for variably saturated flow in porous media [6]. When the divergence of the water flux vector ($\boldsymbol{\nabla} \cdot \textbf{j}_{\text{w}}$) is simplified to only consider vertical water fluxes, this gives:

\[\begin{equation} \phi\frac{\partial \xi(\psi)}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left[\kappa_w \frac{\partial \left(\psi + z\right)}{\partial z}\right] + F_{\text{w}}(z,t) \end{equation}\]

which is called the mixed-form equation, as the rate of change on the left-hand side is in $\xi$ but the vertical gradient on the right-hand side is in $\psi$ [7].

Implicit Richards equation

The Timestepping trait argument of compute_tendencies! for RichardsEq is the resolved timestepping class of saturation_water_ice (see Implicit-explicit time stepping). It is Explicit unless the model is run with an IMEX timestepper, in which case SoilModel and LandModel route the saturation of a Richards-equation hydrology to the implicit sub-stepper:

julia> Terrarium.default_timestepping(SoilHydrology(Float64, NoFlow()))

source
  • Under Explicit(), the tendency contains the full divergence of the darcy_flux, the evapotranspiration sink, the user-defined forcing, and the flux boundary conditions on saturation_water_ice (e.g. InfiltrationFlux).
  • Under Implicit(), the Darcy fluxes through the interior cell faces are omitted, since they are integrated by the implicit sub-stepper. Only the Darcy fluxes through the two boundary faces of the column remain, via compute_boundary_darcy_flux_divergence. These vanish for the default (no-flux) conditions on pressure_head and carry the prescribed flux for a gradient condition on pressure_head (e.g. FreeDrainage).

In both cases, the Darcy fluxes at $t^n$ through all cell faces are stored in the water_flux auxiliary variable (m/s, positive upward, on cell faces; see compute_water_flux). Under Implicit(), the implicit step overwrites the fluxes through the interior faces with those of its conservative update (compute_implicit_water_flux). After a step, water_flux therefore holds the flux that moved the water, with which the soil energy advects heat (see Soil energy balance).

The hydrology closure redistributes over- and undersaturation with adjust_saturation_profile!. It moves the sensible heat $C_\text{w} T$ of the water it shifts between cells in the internal_energy, at the temperature of the cell that the water leaves, and removes that of the excess water it discards (standalone SoilModel) or routes to the surface_excess_water pool (LandModel), which stores no heat. The energy closure, which runs after the hydrology closure, then recomputes the temperature from the adjusted energy. In the implicit Richards step, only the closure of the last iteration moves heat, so the internal energy is adjusted exactly once per step.

The implicit step is the modified Picard iteration of [2] for the mixed form, solved in the total hydraulic head with the lagged face conductivity of the Darcy flux and the lagged specific moisture capacity $C = \partial\theta/\partial\psi$ given by compute_implicit_conductivity! and compute_implicit_capacity!, followed by a conservative update of the saturation and the hydrology closure; see Implicit Richards equation for the scheme. In saturated cells $C = 0$; the minimum_moisture_capacity of the hydraulic properties bounds $C$ from below so that frozen saturated cells, whose hydraulic conductivity vanishes as well, do not make the system singular. Flux boundary conditions on saturation_water_ice and gradient boundary conditions on pressure_head are supported; value boundary conditions on pressure_head are not yet supported.

The terms that stay explicit can remove more water from a cell in one step than it holds, e.g. gravity drainage through the bottom face of a draining sandy column. water_outflow_timescale reports the time in which they would drain a cell to its residual saturation, which bounds the step of a TimeStepWizard through cell_advection_timescale (see Adaptive time stepping with IMEX). It evaluates the explicit terms selected by the resolved class of saturation_water_ice, so under Explicit() it includes the interior Darcy fluxes as well.

The implicit operator of saturation_water_ice is a TridiagonalOperator in the total hydraulic head pressure_head, the variable of the SoilSaturationPressureClosure (see its varname method below), returned by implicit_operator (which also validates the configuration), and each iteration of the implicit solver calls the process hooks of SoilHydrology with RichardsEq below:

Hydraulic properties

Besides the static soil characteristics mentioned in Soil stratigraphy such as porosity ($\phi$), a key time-varying hydraulic property is the (non-saturated) hydraulic conductivity $\kappa_{\text{w}}$. Different ways of modelling $\kappa_{\text{w}}$ are defined as subyptes of AbstractUnsatK. The simplest formulation, called UnsatKLinear, models $\kappa_{\text{w}}$ as:

\[\begin{equation} \kappa_{\text{w}} = \frac{\theta_{\text{liq}}}{\theta + \theta_{\text{air}}} \kappa_{\text{w,sat}}\, \end{equation}\]

with $\kappa_{\text{w,sat}}$ the saturated hydraulic conductivity (m/s). A more complex formulation taking ice into account, called UnsatKVanGenuchten,follows [8], here presented in the form of [9, Eq. (26)]:

\[\begin{equation} \kappa_{\text{w}} = I_\text{ice}\left(\frac{\theta_{\text{liq}}}{\phi}\right)^{0.5} \left[1 - \left(1 - \left(\frac{\theta_{\text{liq}}}{\phi}\right)^{n/(n+1)}\right)^{(n-1)/n}\right]^2 \kappa_{\text{w,sat}}\,, \end{equation}\]

which adds an ice impedance factor $I_\text{ice} = 10^{\Omega\theta_{\text{ice}}/\theta}$ to the classic van Genuchten formulation to account for the blocking of water-filled pores by ice.

Closures

Critical to the modelling of water transport in the soil, is the relationship between soil water/ice saturation ($\xi$) and the matric potential ($\Psi_m$), which is called the soil-water retention curve (SWRC). Recall from the documentation on the core interfaces that the total hydraulic head $\Psi$ is defined as:

\[\begin{equation} \Psi = \Psi_m(\xi) + \Psi_z + \Psi_h \end{equation}\]

with $\Psi_z$ the elevation head and $\Psi_h$ the hydrostatic head contributed by free water above the water table. Currently, two implementations of the SWRC are available in Terrarium via FreezeCurves.jl: the Brooks-Corey (BrooksCorey) and van Genuchten (VanGenuchten) formulations.

Represents a closure relating saturation of water/ice in soil pores to a corresponding pressure (or hydraulic) head. This relation is typically described by soil property-dependent soil-water retention curve (SWRC) which is here defined in implementations of AbstractSoilHydraulics.

Part of the Darcy flux divergence at i, j, k due to the two boundary faces of the column (bottom face of cell k = 1, top face of cell k = Nz), omitting all interior faces:

(δ_{k,Nz} q_{k+½} - δ_{k,1} q_{k-½}) / Δz_k,

with q the darcy_flux. This is the explicit part when the interior fluxes are integrated implicitly (see Picard). The boundary fluxes are computed from the pressure_head halo: they vanish for the default (no-flux) conditions and carry the prescribed flux for a gradient condition on pressure_head. Kernel function that adjusts saturation profiles to account for oversaturation and undersaturation arising due to numerical error. This implementation scans over the saturation profiles at each lateral grid cell and redistributes excess water upward layer-by-layer until reaching the topmost layer. The remaining surface excess is returned as the excess saturation of the topmost layer times its thickness (m); the caller multiplies by the topmost porosity to get a water depth (m³/m²) to route into a surface water pool, or discards it. With an energy process, every saturation change also changes the internal energy in out by the sensible heat of the water (compute_redistributed_heat!).
